## Bounded profiling: is the extra iteration budget costing typical hours, or a rare-case minority?

Per the task's explicit step 5, and using only the **always-on**, default-build day-boundary `final_hour_elapsed_ms` line (no `--verbose-diagnostics` rebuild needed -- a methodological improvement over `run-006`, which needed a temporary instrumentation addition for this):

- **Steady region (hours 1-2,568, 2,568 hours)**: day-boundary elapsed values are consistently in the tens-of-milliseconds range (e.g., 40-90 ms typical), with a mild, regularly-recurring ~200-400 ms spike roughly every 240 hours (visible well before hour 2,578 too, e.g. at hours 240, 480, 720, 960, 1200, 1440, 1680, 1920, 2160) -- this periodic pattern is not new or related to the fix batch; it appears throughout the whole steady region at a stable magnitude.
- **Post-checkpoint region (hours 2,569-2,893, 325 hours, repeat 1's single full-tail sample)**: three narrow clusters of `SoluteReactionSolverDidNotConverge` recovery-escalation warnings were found (at substep counts 1, 16, and 20), located (by cross-referencing surrounding day-boundary markers) within approximately hours 2,569-2,592, hours 2,665-2,688, and hours 2,785-2,808 -- **not spread uniformly across all 325 hours**. The single most expensive hour anywhere in the entire run is **hour 2,592: 15,329 ms** -- more than 65x the largest steady-region spike, and by itself comparable in magnitude to the entire rest of the 2,568-hour steady window's *typical* per-hour cost summed over dozens of hours. A second elevated hour, **2,808: 2,265 ms**, is also well outside the steady region's normal range.
- **Conclusion**: the raised iteration ceiling's extra cost is **not** a broad, typical-hour tax -- ordinary hours remain as cheap as before the fix batch. It is a **concentrated, rare-case cost**, exactly as the task's own framing anticipated as the alternative to "typical hours got slower" -- but it is a *larger* rare-case cost than the old ceiling produced, because the solver is now permitted to grind for up to 200 (vs. 60) iterations per attempt before giving up, and it is doing so at several specific hard hours in the 2,568-2,893 stretch that the old, lower ceiling would have failed out of much faster (or, before `issue-015`'s fix, the run simply never got past hour 2,578 to reach them at all).
