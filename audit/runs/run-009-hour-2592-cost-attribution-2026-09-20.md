## Why hour 2,592 specifically is expensive: inherited substep retention, not its own escalation

Reading `updateAcceptedRecoveryPreference` (`hourly_heat_water_solute.zig:11778-11804`) and `coarsening_probe_cooldown_hours=3` (`:11687`) resolves exactly which hours this affects. After hour 2,589's escalation-triggered acceptance at substep=20, `coarsening_probe_cooldown` resets to 3 and `preferred_substep_count` is pinned at 20 ("the accepted schedule is retained briefly as a prior-timestep predictor", per the function's own doc comment at `:11840-11855`). The cooldown then decrements on each subsequent clean hour:

| hour | cooldown before | action | substep_count run at |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2,590 | 3 | decrement to 2, stays at 20 | 20 |
| 2,591 | 2 | decrement to 1, stays at 20 | 20 |
| **2,592** | 1 | decrement to 0, **computes the coarser probe target (16) for hour 2,593** | **20** |

This is arithmetically exact (3 -> 2 -> 1 -> 0 across exactly 3 hours: 2,590/2,591/2,592), not inferred from timing alone. **Hour 2,592 itself never appears in an hour-level `bounded fixed external hour recovery rejected` line in this window** -- it does not escalate on its own. It is expensive because it is the *last* hour of a 3-hour retention window inherited from hour 2,589's stiffness event 3 hours earlier, during which every hour -- including 2,592 -- must run the whole external hour as **20 internal substeps instead of 1**, each substep performing its own full SOLUTE Newton/Anderson reaction-network solve. Per `run-006`'s already-established, still-holding finding, that SOLUTE phase is 85-97% of any attempt's cost and its cost is driven by iteration count, not substep count directly -- consistent with this window's one visible internal retry needing 85+ iterations for a single substep while ordinary substeps cost tens of milliseconds. Summing ~20 substep closures with this kind of iteration-count variance is fully consistent with the observed 14,185 ms total (confirmed independently by `run-008`'s and `run-007`'s cross-session reproduction of the same ~14-16 s figure for this exact hour). Confirming the window closes cleanly: hour 2,616 (day 109's boundary, after the coarsening probe at hour 2,593 presumably succeeded) is back to a normal **83 ms**, showing this is a genuinely transient, localized cost, not a lasting regime change.

## Answering the task's explicit sub-question: is the raised 60->200 SOLUTE budget a major contributor to hour 2,592 itself?

**No, not for hour 2,592 specifically.** The one internal SOLUTE retry visibly attributable to the 2,590-2,592 window used only 85 of its 200-iteration ceiling (42.5%) before self-recovering -- it never approached, let alone exhausted, the budget. Budget exhaustion (151+49=200, exactly the ceiling) *did* drive the cost of the escalating hour 2,573, three hours earlier -- but that is a different hour with a different, hour-level failure/escalation event, not hour 2,592. For hour 2,592 itself, the cost driver is **the sheer volume of substep-repeated SOLUTE closures** (20x instead of 1x, inherited via the ladder's own documented "predictor retention" design) combined with ordinary per-substep Newton/Anderson iteration cost -- not the iteration ceiling. This directly refines (rather than contradicts) `run-008`'s finding that the extra iteration budget's cost "concentrates in a handful of hard hours": the hours where the *budget itself* binds are the escalating hours (2,573, 2,589, and by the same pattern presumably 2,856/2,880/2,808), while hour 2,592's cost is a second-order consequence of the ladder's retention/cooldown mechanism, not the budget size.

## Classification

**(a): legitimate, ladder-designed work.** The retention-and-cooldown mechanism (`updateAcceptedRecoveryPreference`, `coarsening_probe_cooldown_hours`) is the approved bounded-fallback architecture working exactly as documented ("the accepted schedule is retained briefly as a prior-timestep predictor, then clean hours probe one coarser supported schedule") -- not a bug, not runaway/unbounded retry (it is a fixed 3-hour window, not the contract's prohibited "unbounded 2/4/8/16/... retry cascade"), and not redundant computation: each of the 20 substep closures within hour 2,592 solves a genuinely different sub-hour state, so re-solving is not "the same work twice." No evidence of (b) an algorithm/data-structure defect specific to this hour's state (no unusual O(n^2) pattern found; the cost scales with iteration count exactly as `run-006` already characterized generally) or (c) redundant/duplicate computation (no repeated identical closure calls found; `run-006`'s prior audit of the retry path's own plumbing -- transactional snapshots, per-attempt logging -- already found and closed this line of inquiry with a negative result, and nothing in this run's reading of the retention/cooldown code contradicts that).

## Safe-fix search and disposition

Per the same boundary `run-006` already established (not attempting to change convergence/retention *behavior*, only looking for behavior-neutral waste): the retention/cooldown window's own bookkeeping (`updateAcceptedRecoveryPreference`, `nextCoarserRecoverySubstepCount`, `boundedInitialRecoverySubstepCount`) is a handful of scalar comparisons and array scans over an 8-element array -- negligible cost next to the multi-second SOLUTE solves it gates. Shortening the 3-hour cooldown or skipping the retention window would change *when* the model probes back down to a coarser (cheaper but previously-shown-insufficient) substep schedule -- a convergence-behavior change requiring its own accuracy evidence, explicitly out of scope for a performance pass and prohibited by the project contract's "internal iterations/substeps already belonging to an approved method must be documented and bounded... must not change this policy silently" clause. **No safe, output-neutral optimization was found.** This is an honest negative result: hour 2,592's cost is real, physics/solver-driven work (repeated Newton/Anderson reaction-network solves at an inherited elevated substep count), not waste, and should not be spun as a fix opportunity.
