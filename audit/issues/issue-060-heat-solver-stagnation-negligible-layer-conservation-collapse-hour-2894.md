## Addendum (2026-09-19, follow-up diagnosis pass): mechanism identified via source trace, not yet independently confirmed by rerun

Per the contract's investigation order (inputs/bindings before physics) and three-experiment budget. This pass performed source-level tracing (reading, not running) as experiment 1; it explicitly declines a full rerun (see "Why no rerun" below) rather than spend the budget on a non-cheap repeat of an already-observed failure.

### Hypothesis

The heat-solver stagnation and the elemental-mass collapse are not two independent problems that happen to coincide -- they are two symptoms of the **same shared, unguarded state variable**: `grid.matrix_liquid_water_m3[layer]` for cell 0's soil layer 0. This is read directly by both:

1. The heat solver's negligible-capacity discard check, `ecosys-ng/src/soil/water/heat_step.zig:3332-3345` -- `layer_total_heat_capacity_megajoules_per_k` is built from `dry_solid_heat_capacity` plus `liquid_water_heat_capacity_megajoules_per_m3_k * (grid.matrix_liquid_water_m3[layer] + macropore + vapor)`. At hour 2,894 this fell to `2.03e-5 MJ/K`, four orders of magnitude under the `8.38e-5` `STARTS 655` floor (`ecosys-ng/src/soil/heat/flux.zig:47-62`), so the layer's heat update was discarded (`heat_step.zig:3347` warning) rather than solved.
2. The soil per-layer mass-inventory reconstruction used by the very conservation gate that rejected the hour, `ecosys-ng/src/validation/landscape_mass_inventory_phosphorus_ions.zig`. `aggregateProfilePhosphorusAndIonsLayer` is called from `layer_mass_inventory.zig:93-106` with `inputs.grid.matrix_liquid_water_m3` passed straight through as `soil_water_m3` -- the identical array, indexed by the identical `layer`. Inside, `water_m3 = try aqueousCarrierM3(soil_water_m3[profile_cell], chemistry.dry_reference_water_m3[profile_cell])` (`landscape_mass_inventory_phosphorus_ions.zig:251-254, 567-572`), and `water_m3` is then used as the extensive carrier for the **solid/mineral-precipitate** concentration fields via `extensiveGeochemistrySolids(solids, water_m3)` (line 355, helper at 574-579) -- gibbsite, iron hydroxide, calcite, gypsum, aluminum/iron/calcium phosphate, and aluminum/iron/calcium "natural"/"ground" silicate (`geochemistrySolidElements`, line ~581 onward). These solid/mineral pools are the dominant real-world reservoir for Al, Fe, Ca, Mg, Na, K and Si in soil -- far larger than the aqueous or exchange-phase fractions of the same elements, which is exactly the element set that collapsed.

`aqueousCarrierM3`'s own comment (`landscape_mass_inventory_phosphorus_ions.zig:565-566`) cites `solute.f:610`: the legacy oracle keeps the extensive `Z*` value once `VOLW <= ZEROS2` (i.e. water below a small threshold), and the Zig translation's guard is `if (live_water_m3 > 0) live_water_m3 else dry_reference_water_m3` -- **it only substitutes the stable reference volume when water is EXACTLY zero.** It has no floor for water that has collapsed to a tiny-but-nonzero value, unlike the legacy `ZEROS2` threshold it is supposed to translate, and unlike the heat solver's own `minimum_layer_heat_capacity_megajoules_per_m2_k` floor for the same layer.

If layer 0's water content shrinks sharply (but not to exact zero) during the pathological heat-solver stagnation/recovery episode -- plausible given the same hour's `implausible surface conductive flux` sequence shows a large, oscillating surface/subsurface temperature split at cell 0 across the forced 20-substep recovery ladder (`SURFACE-HEAT-BRACKET-RUNAWAY-001`/`DRY-LAYER-UNPHYSICAL-HEAT-SINK-HOUR-2726-001`) -- then the "after" mass-inventory snapshot multiplies essentially unchanged solid-mineral concentrations by a much smaller carrier than the "before" snapshot did. The ledger faithfully reports the resulting drop as a loss with `internal_production=0`/`internal_consumption=0`, because no modeled transport or reaction actually ran on that mass; the loss is an artifact of the carrier term, not physics.

### Evidence for this hypothesis (read from the actual run's log and evidence journal, not fabricated)

Recovered the referenced run artifacts from this session's scratchpad (`production-run-2026-09-19.log`, `production-run-2026-09-19-evidence.json`) and re-read the exact failing sequence (log lines 2506-2583):

- Line 2508-2512: heat stagnation at `cell=0`, candidate temperature range `179.5 K`-`284.7 K`, `defect_mj=-8.5` vs `tolerance_mj=1.49e-10`.
- Line 2517: `bounded fixed external hour recovery accepted: exact_substep_count=20` -- the hour is nominally recovered.
- Line 2527: `negligible-capacity layer discarded heat ... cell=0 layer=0 total_heat_capacity_megajoules_per_k=2.03e-5 limit_megajoules_per_k=8.38e-5 discarded_megajoules=-14.7`.
- Lines 2547-2557 (the per-cell/instantaneous-scale block, cell=0): every failing quantity shows `internal_production=0e0 internal_consumption=0e0` and an `after` value that lands suspiciously close to that hour's tiny `external_inputs`, i.e. the reported "after" pool looks like "whatever small amount is freshly attributable" rather than "before plus a net physical change":
  - `silicon`: `before=100.136 after=0.0024495`, `external_inputs=0.0024495` -- **after and external_inputs agree to 5 significant figures.**
  - `iron`: `before=35.861 after=0.0017586`, `external_inputs=0.00087280` (after ~ 2x inputs).
  - `magnesium`/`sodium`/`potassium`: `after` each `~1.7x` their own `external_inputs`.
  - `aluminum`/`calcium`: `after` less tightly tied to `external_inputs` (factor ~30x and ~460x respectively) -- consistent with these two elements also carrying meaningful exchange-phase and phosphate-mineral mass that is *not* gated by the same collapsing water carrier, diluting the effect relative to Fe/Si/Mg/Na/K, whose modeled reservoirs in this deck are apparently almost entirely the water-referenced solid/mineral fields.
  - The landscape-level block (lines 2528-2538, values in the thousands) shows the *same absolute loss* per element as the per-cell block (e.g. aluminum landscape loss `10172.7 -> 10134.2` = `-38.5`, matching the per-cell `38.54 -> 0.031` = `-38.51` almost exactly) -- confirming cell 0 alone accounts for essentially all of the landscape's loss this hour, consistent with a single-cell, single-layer degenerate state rather than a distributed effect.

### Reachability / materiality assessment

This is **not** hour-2,894-specific or weather-event-specific in its root cause. The precondition chain is:
1. A soil layer's total heat capacity drifts at/below the `STARTS 655` floor (a function of that layer becoming thin/dry -- a recurring, not one-off, soil-drying condition; the same tag family was already seen non-fatally at hours 2,705-2,726 under a different substep/iteration configuration per issue-006).
2. The heat Newton-Raphson/Anderson solver stagnates on that layer and falls through the substep-escalation recovery ladder.
3. The same layer's `matrix_liquid_water_m3` ends the hour collapsed-but-nonzero rather than exactly zero.
4. The mass-inventory ledger's `aqueousCarrierM3` guard (exact-zero only) fails to substitute the stable reference volume, so the solid-mineral-dominated elements (Al, Fe, Ca, Mg, Na, K, Si) are undercounted in the "after" snapshot relative to "before."

None of steps 1-4 depend on the specific date/weather of hour 2,894 -- they depend only on a layer reaching a degenerate thin/dry state, which the project's own history shows recurring (2,705-2,726, then 2,894, under different configurations). This should be treated as a **general vulnerability that can recur at other hours**, not a one-off tied to day 121. It is also the first confirmed case where the pre-existing, previously-"cosmetic" `SURFACE-HEAT-BRACKET-RUNAWAY-001`/`DRY-LAYER-UNPHYSICAL-HEAT-SINK-HOUR-2726-001` gap is load-bearing for whether the full 262,920-hour run can ever finish, since any recurrence at a later hour will hit the same unguarded carrier.

### Assessment of fix scope (diagnosis only -- no fix applied this pass)

The candidate fix is narrow and clean in shape, similar in spirit to issue-015's iteration-floor fix: broaden `aqueousCarrierM3`'s guard from `live_water_m3 > 0` to a floor-based test (e.g. reuse the layer's own physical-tolerance water floor already computed elsewhere, such as `context.config.physical_tolerance.waterVolume(bulk_volume_m3)` used at `soil_chemistry_convergence.zig:366`, or a floor consistent with the legacy `ZEROS2` threshold `aqueousCarrierM3`'s own comment cites). However, unlike issue-015's fix, this one is **not** independently confirmed by a rerun in this pass, and choosing the right floor value needs a legacy-parity check against `solute.f:610`'s actual `ZEROS2` magnitude before it is applied -- this pass recommends it as a strong, well-scoped candidate for a fast, dedicated follow-up, not as a fix to apply blind. This issue stays `NOT_ASSESSED`/`OPEN` until a fix is proposed, reviewed against the legacy threshold, and validated by a rerun or targeted unit reproduction.
