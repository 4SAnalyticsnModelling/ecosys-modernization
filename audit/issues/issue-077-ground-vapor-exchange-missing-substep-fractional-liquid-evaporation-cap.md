## Mechanism (source-verified both sides)

### Fortran: `EVAPGW` is capped by an explicit per-substep FRACTION of available water, not just by availability

`f77src/watsub.f:2860-2865`:
```
      EVAPGX=PAREGM*(VPQG(M,NY,NX)-VP1)
      EVAPGV=AMAX1(EVAPGX,
     2-AMAX1(0.0,VOLV2(NUM(NY,NX),NY,NX)))
      EVAPGW=AMAX1(EVAPGX-EVAPGV
     2,-AMAX1(0.0,VOLW2(NUM(NY,NX),NY,NX))*XNPHX)
      EVAPG(NY,NX)=EVAPGV+EVAPGW
```
`EVAPGX` is the unlimited atmospheric-demand term (`PAREGM`, a conductance, times the vapor-concentration gradient). `EVAPGV` caps the vapor-phase leg at currently-available vapor (`VOLV2`). `EVAPGW` -- the liquid-water leg, the one that actually depletes soil water -- is capped not merely at "don't exceed available water" but at `-VOLW2(...)*XNPHX`: **available liquid water multiplied by `XNPHX`, an explicit extra fractional-rate limiter**, on top of whatever fraction of the hour the demand term (`PAREGM`) itself already represents.

`XNPHX` is defined once per hour at `f77src/wthr.f:604-618`: `XNFH=1.0/NFH`, `XNPH=1.0/NPH`, `XNPHX=XNPH*XNFH` -- i.e. `XNPHX = 1/(NFH*NPH)`, the reciprocal of the *total* compounded substep count for this hour (already established by `issue-024` round 1/2 as `NFH=4` fixed, `NPH>=20` for a thin/low-heat-capacity top layer like this one, giving `XNPHX<=1/80` in the finest case, or `<=1/4` in the coarsest). Separately, `PAREGM`'s own demand-side conductance already bakes in the *same* `XNPHX` scaling via `PAREX(NY,NX)=AREA(3,NU(NY,NX),NY,NX)*XNPHX` (`f77src/hour1.f:168`) -- so the demand term and the availability cap both scale with the substep fraction, but the cap applies **an independent, second application of that fraction specifically to how much of the standing liquid-water pool can be removed**, regardless of how large the raw demand is. Consequence: even under unbounded atmospheric demand, one substep's `EVAPGW` can remove at most an `XNPHX` fraction of the water present at that substep's start; the worst-case total depletion compounded over a full hour's substeps is bounded by `1-(1-XNPHX)^(NFH*NPH)`, which approaches `1-e^-1~=63%` as substep count grows, and is far smaller in the common case where demand does not saturate the cap every substep. This is a deliberate numerical/physical throttle, structurally identical in spirit to a CFL-type stability limiter, and it is the reason Fortran's layer 1 can dry out over hundreds of hours but never in one.

### Zig: `ground_vapor_exchange.zig`'s cap is "don't go negative," with no fractional-rate term

`ecosys-ng/src/surface/ground_vapor_exchange.zig:50-69` (`splitVaporThenLiquid`):
```
    const vapor_change = @max(unlimited_water_change_m3, -owner_vapor_water_equivalent_m3);
    const liquid_remainder = unlimited_water_change_m3 - vapor_change;
    const liquid_change = @max(liquid_remainder, -owner_liquid_water_m3);
```
This is the direct structural analog of `EVAPGV`/`EVAPGW`: `vapor_change` mirrors `EVAPGV` (capped at available vapor), `liquid_change` mirrors `EVAPGW` (capped at available liquid). But the liquid cap here is `-owner_liquid_water_m3` -- **100% of available water**, with no `* XNPHX`-equivalent fractional term at all. `:108-116` (`accepted`) computes `unlimited_water_change_m3 = vapor_conductance_m3_per_h * (air_vapor_volume_fraction - owner_vapor_volume_fraction) * time_step_hours` -- the demand-side `time_step_hours` scaling is present and structurally matches Fortran's `PAREGM`/`PAREX` demand-side `XNPHX` scaling -- but the *second*, independent fractional throttle on the size of the liquid-water sink itself, which Fortran applies specifically to `EVAPGW`, has no counterpart anywhere in this function or its caller.

Call site: `ecosys-ng/src/stages/hourly_heat_water_solute.zig:6688-6702` passes `time_step_hours = time_step_hours` (whatever the hour's currently-accepted external/substep duration is) and `owner_liquid_water_m3 = grid.matrix_liquid_water_m3[top]` (the layer's actual current liquid water) straight into `accepted`, with no additional caller-side rate limiting either.

**Net effect**: whenever atmospheric evaporative demand for this cell/layer is large enough that `unlimited_water_change_m3` (in magnitude) exceeds the layer's *entire* currently-available liquid water for the substep being evaluated, Zig's cap permits removing all of it in that one substep/step, with no throttle preventing the layer from going to (near) zero within a single external hour. Fortran's structurally-analogous cap never permits more than an `XNPHX` fraction of the pool to be removed per substep, regardless of how large demand is -- which is exactly why Fortran's layer 1 shows a smooth multi-hundred-hour decline while Zig's shows a single-hour cliff.
