## Task framing (why this is a fresh angle, not a repeat)

Per this task's explicit instructions, three avenues were considered before running anything: (1) the water retention curve's anchor-point precision for this layer's specific FC=0.28/WP=0.15 parameters (`ecosys-ng/src/soil/water/retention.zig`); (2) a systematic bias in the evaporation *demand* formula itself, not its already-fixed rate cap (`ecosys-ng/src/surface/ground_vapor_exchange.zig`, `ecosys-ng/src/surface/gas_boundary_conductance.zig`); (3) infiltration partitioning between layer 1 and deeper layers.

Source reading on (1) found the production Mualem-van Genuchten fit (`retention.zig`'s `fitOriginalMualemVanGenuchten`) algebraically solves `residual_water_content_m3_per_m3` to exactly reproduce the field-capacity anchor and Newton-iterates `n` to a `1e-8`-`1e-10` tolerance against the wilting-point anchor -- tight enough that anchor-reproduction error alone is very unlikely to explain a divergence of the observed magnitude. Source reading on (2) found `gas_boundary_conductance.zig`'s `calculateSurfaceExchange`/`calculate` are a faithful, structurally matching translation of `watsub.f`'s `PAREGM`/`RAGS`/`RAE`/`RAGE` resistance chain (including the `RAGE=RAG*THETPG` soil-gas-diffusion term, which the Zig side reproduces as `soil_evaporation_resistance = soil_evaporation_pore_resistance_h_per_m * soil_surface_air_fraction`) -- no obvious formula-level bias found by inspection. Avenue (3) is where the one bounded experiment below produced a genuinely new, concrete signature.

The run was allowed to progress for ~2 minutes (reaching hour ~2198) and then killed -- well inside the ~25-minute budget for this check, since only hours 1-1000 were needed. Every one of layers 1-6 rises by roughly +0.04 to +0.12 (absolute volumetric fraction) over this ~900-hour, ~37-day window. Layer 12 (deepest, established separately by `feature-019`'s already-captured data for this exact deck/run family) stays essentially flat and continues to track the Fortran oracle to ~1e-5-1e-7 relative difference through this same era. **This rules out a layer-1-only mechanism** (e.g. a layer-1-specific retention-curve or evaporation-demand defect) as the sole explanation -- whatever is happening reaches at least six layers deep but does not (yet, by hour 1000) reach layer 12.

**Result 2 -- ice is not the driver for this window (ruling out a repeat of issue-024's hour-1 mechanism)**: `volumetric_ice_fraction_layer_2` through `_layer_6` are all ~0 (1e-17 to 1e-23, numerical noise) from hour ~300 onward, confirmed at hours 300/500/700/900/1000. The rise in Result 1 is a genuine LIQUID-water increase, not a symptom of the Dall'Amico freeze-thaw over-conversion issue-024 rounds 1-8 already diagnosed for hour 1 (that mechanism is ice/liquid-partition-driven and concentrated at hour 1's cold snap; this window's ice content is already negligible).

**Result 3 -- neither evapotranspiration nor runoff can account for the gain, and runoff is exactly zero throughout**: cumulative `evapotranspiration[mm]` summed over hours 1-1000 is **1.224 mm** -- two orders of magnitude too small to be the compensating loss for a gain of this size. Cumulative `runoff[mm]` summed over the identical 1000 hours is **exactly 0.0 mm** (every single hour's value is `0e0`). Meanwhile `surface_water_equivalent[mm]` (snowpack SWE) itself grows from ~7 mm (hour 100) to ~125 mm (hour 1000) -- i.e. the snowpack is accumulating, not melting, over this window, so the soil-layer water gain cannot be explained by snowmelt draining out of a shrinking snowpack either. Whatever water is entering layers 1-6, its source is not visible in this output stream's own runoff/evapotranspiration/snowmelt accounting.

## What this is and is not

- **Not the same mechanism as issue-024's rounds 1-8.** Those rounds are entirely about hour 1's large, ice-dominated single-hour conversion at a specific cold snap. This signature is a slow, ~37-day, ice-negligible, multi-layer liquid-water creep with no single dramatic event.
- **Not the same mechanism as issue-077.** That issue restored a missing per-substep *fractional evaporation-rate cap* that only matters when demand would otherwise strip an entire layer's water in one substep (confirmed inert for hours where the coarse first attempt succeeds, e.g. hour 2894). This window's evapotranspiration is already near-zero; a rate cap on removal cannot explain water that is *arriving*, not failing to leave fast enough.
- **Not the same mechanism as issue-078.** That issue is a rare (twice in 3,252 hours), tillage-triggered, single-event pore-capacity overflow at soil-index layer 1 (Fortran's "layer 2"). This signature spans hours 100-1000, hundreds of hours before the first tillage event (hour 2,532 per `stage_execution_census`), and affects layers 1-6 together, not a tillage mixing zone.
- **Not yet proven to be a defect rather than legitimate physics.** This pass did **not** obtain a matching Fortran-side trajectory for layers 2-6 over the identical hours 100-1000 window (only layer 1 and layer 12 have already-captured Fortran comparison points, from `feature-019`); it is possible (though it would need to be shown, not assumed) that the Fortran oracle's own mid-profile layers also rise somewhat during a winter recharge period from residual autumn moisture redistribution. What is unusual and worth flagging regardless is the *combination*: multi-layer synchronized rise, zero runoff for 1000 consecutive hours despite a substantial and growing snowpack, and evapotranspiration two orders of magnitude too small to balance it -- a combination that, at minimum, deserves its own targeted comparison rather than being folded into issue-024's hour-1-specific diagnosis.
